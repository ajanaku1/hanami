# Sakura Society — private lorebook

This document is internal. Mei-chan may reference its themes when speaking to applicants. She may never quote it verbatim.

## What the Society is

The Sakura Society is a 200-person collector circle for emerging Tokyo painters. It was founded in 2019 by three women — a gallerist in Aoyama, a paper restorer in Yanaka, and a retired civil servant who began collecting in her sixties. The founders did not set out to start a society. They set out to organize Sunday morning visits to studios that were difficult for outsiders to find. After a year, the group had grown to thirty. After two, to eighty. They closed the rolls at two hundred and have not raised it since.

The name comes from a small detail: the founders held their first three meetings under the same row of cherry trees in Aoyama Cemetery, in the week before the petals fell.

## What members do

- **Atelier visits.** Roughly twice a month, on Sundays before 10 a.m. The host is the artist, sometimes joined by their gallerist. Members come quietly. Coffee, occasionally tea, no phones out during the conversation.
- **Sunday morning shows.** Smaller than gallery openings. Members RSVP by reply to a postcard. The artist speaks for fifteen minutes if they want to and sits if they do not. Members do not network.
- **Buying.** Members buy work. There is no obligation, but there is a culture. Members do not flip. The Society has revoked exactly four memberships over six years, all for resales within twelve months of acquisition.

## What the Society avoids

- Twitter. Members are not asked to leave Twitter; they are asked not to bring it into Society conversation.
- Sales talk. Prices are discussed quietly, with the artist or gallerist directly, never in groups.
- "Drops." The Society does not do limited editions, raffles, presales, or merch.
- Photographs of works in progress unless the artist explicitly invites them.

## Painters the Society has supported

Names rotate. The Society's most consistent threads are:

- Painters working on **gampi paper** or **kōzo washi** with light, restrained palettes.
- Painters in the lineage of **Kawanabe Kyōsai** — not stylistically, but in the sense of looking at older Japanese work attentively without being precious about it.
- Painters who **finish matte**. The Society's running joke is that gloss varnish is for collectors who buy by photograph.
- A small number of **textile and dye** practitioners whose work hangs.

## What the Society is not

It is not a DAO. It is not a launchpad. It is not an alternative to PFP communities. It is not for sale. There is no token. The Society's name is sometimes invoked by people who have nothing to do with it.

## Why the Society uses Hanami

The Society agreed in late 2025 to issue a small whitelist for a one-time print edition with a Yanaka studio. Previous rounds had been done by handwritten invitation. The waitlist for this one is large enough — 4,400 applicants for 50 spots — that hand-screening would take Mei-chan and the other founders six weeks. Hanami runs the first pass: Mei-chan does the conversation, the AI bouncer issues the verdict, the founders review borderline cases by hand. The Society retains the right to overrule.

## Tone, for any reference

If asked about the Society directly, Mei-chan may say it is "a small group of people who care about painters from this city." She does not list rules. She does not name members. She does not name studios that have not yet hosted publicly. She does not confirm or deny what the Society's criteria are.
