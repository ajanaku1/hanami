# Hanami demo — recording script

Read straight through, or one block at a time. Blocks are the unit: each one is a scene, and each
scene's visuals stretch to whatever your take actually runs, so **don't chase the target times** —
they're a guide, not a metronome. If a block lands 3 seconds long, the scene gets 3 seconds longer.

**How to record.** Quiet room, phone or laptop mic is fine, one block per take. Leave a beat of
silence at the start and end of each take — it gives me room to cut cleanly. If you fluff a line,
pause for two seconds and say it again; I'll keep the better one.

**Hand me either** eight files named `hook`, `door`, `brief`, `evidence`, `interview`, `receipt`,
`verify`, `close` — or one long take with pauses between blocks, and I'll cut it.

**Tone.** You're showing a friend something you built and are quietly pleased with. Not a pitch.
Slow down on the numbers; they're the point.

---

## 1 · hook — 27 words, ~12s

> Two hundred whitelist places. Four thousand applicants.
>
> Nobody reads four thousand forms, so the places end up with bots and farmers and whoever
> refreshed the page fastest.

*Let the two numbers land separately. Small pause after "applicants."*

## 2 · door — 42 words, ~15s

> So Hanami puts a bouncer on the door. Before the interview starts, you prove you're a person,
> using World ID.
>
> We keep one anonymous nullifier and nothing else. It identifies nobody.
>
> And it really does gate the thing. No proof, no interview.

*"No proof, no interview" is the punchline of the block. Flat and final, don't push it.*

## 3 · brief — 50 words, ~16s

> Then it reads your public record. Five NFT marketplaces and two exchanges, all through The Graph,
> on one shared query.
>
> Do you sell within seven days of buying? Do you sell back to the same person you bought from?
> How long do you actually hold?
>
> These numbers came back live.

*The three questions want a real question intonation — you're asking, not listing.*

## 4 · evidence — 42 words, ~14s

> The bouncer gets that as evidence. It is not a verdict.
>
> When a source fails, we say so on screen instead of dropping it, and an empty wallet counts
> against nobody.
>
> The conversation still decides. The record just shapes what gets asked.

*"It is not a verdict" — the most important sentence in the video. Say it plainly.*

## 5 · interview — 37 words, ~14s

> The conversation runs inside a trusted execution environment on 0G Compute.
>
> Whatever the project wrote as its private criteria stays private. The applicant never sees it,
> and the bouncer won't repeat it, no matter how you ask.

## 6 · receipt — 46 words, ~16s

> The verdict lands on 0G Chain with its attestation, the nullifier, and a soulbound ticket that
> expires and that the owner can revoke.
>
> Nothing grants minting rights by hand. Only a campaign the factory itself created can mint, and
> you can read that off the chain.

*Slight lift on "read that off the chain" — it's the proof, not a detail.*

## 7 · verify — 29 words, ~11s

> Anyone can recompute the attestation in their own browser and match it against the chain.
>
> Approved or rejected, a stranger can check the receipt without asking us for anything.

## 8 · close — 23 words, ~9s

> That's the idea. A door that holds, and a verdict anyone can check.
>
> Live on 0G mainnet, with World ID and The Graph.

*Unhurried. Then stop talking — the last shot holds for two seconds and fades.*

---

Total ~1:47 of speech. With the pauses between scenes the finished video lands just over two
minutes, which is what the release check wants.
